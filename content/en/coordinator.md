## Before you start

The Program Operator portal is a web page for your computer. As a Program Coordinator you see the case work only: the worklist, the cases, enrolments, nurse visits, near expirations and your notifications. Programmes, products, networks, place allocations and portal users are set up by your Operator Admin — see the [Operator admin guide](operator.html).

- **Your sign-in.** Your Operator Admin creates your account: a phone number and a password. It is yours alone — every decision you take is recorded under your name.
- **Which cases you see.** The cases of the programmes you are assigned to.
- **One rule to remember.** Opening a case locks it to you, and a release timer frees it again. Nothing is saved until you press a decision button, so anything you leave half-done is lost when you leave the case or the timer runs out.

> [!note] Patient data is shown only where the work needs it. National ID numbers are always partly hidden until you choose to show them.

## Sign in

::shot sign-in | The sign-in screen.
1. **Phone number** — the phone number of your account.
2. **Password** — your password.

4. **Sign in** opens your case worklist.
5. Switch the screen between English and العربية at any time.
6. **How to use this portal** opens the user guides in a new tab.

## Find your way around

::shot portal | The menu and the top bar.
1. **Cases** — the case worklist, your home screen.
2. **Enrol a patient** — shown only if you are allowed to enrol patients yourself.
3. **Nurse visits** — the home-nurse visits of your cases.
4. **Near expirations** — patients to call about boxes close to their expiry date.
5. **Notifications** — the red dot counts the unread ones.
6. The sun switches between a light and a dark screen.
7. **Settings** — choose the **Language**, open this **User guide**, or **Sign out**. Always sign out when you leave a shared computer.

## The case worklist

::shot worklist | The case worklist, on New requests.
1. Search all cases, in every queue, by patient or reference.
2. The queues. The number on each tab is how many cases wait there.
3. Search inside the open tab only — by patient, case reference, programme, doctor or coupon.
4. Click a patient's name, or anywhere on the row, to open the case.
5. Click a column title with arrows to sort by it. The newest enrolments come first.
6. **Status** — where the case stands (see the table below).
7. **Ownership** — who is working on the case right now.

| Tab | What waits there |
|---|---|
| **New requests** | Cases whose documents have just arrived, and new cases whose enrolment form still has to be completed. |
| **Under validation** | Cases being checked, cases sent back to the patient for a correction, and resubmitted cases. |
| **Approved** | Approved cases. They stay here while their doses are being collected. |
| **Rejected** | Rejected cases. Rejection is final. |
| **Follow-ups** | Follow-up results sent by the doctor that wait for your review. |
| **Extension requests** | Patients who asked for more time to upload their documents. |
| **Expiring soon** | Approved coupons that expire tomorrow. On this tab the date column shows **Coupon expires**. |

**Back to worklist** on a case returns you to the tab and page you opened it from. Sorting orders the cases on the page you are looking at. With the keyboard, **↑ ↓** move between cases and **Enter** opens one.

::shot worklist-ownership | Ownership and markers, on Under validation.
1. **With …** — another coordinator has this case open. You can read it but not act on it.
2. **Unowned** — nobody has it; open it to take it. Your own cases show **Yours**.
3. **Correction requested** — sent back to the patient; waiting for them.
4. **Resubmitted** — the patient sent the corrected documents; it is ready for you again.
5. **Another programme** — the patient also has a live case on another programme of your office.
6. The shield and number count the open abuse signals on the case.

> [!tip] A red **Awaiting documents** badge with *Not ready to review* means the patient has not sent their documents yet — there is nothing to validate.

### Find a case in any queue

When you do not know which queue a case is in, type in the search box at the top of the page: a patient's name, a case reference, a programme, a doctor or a coupon number.

::shot worklist-search | Searching every queue at once.
1. The search term. The **×** (or the Esc key) clears it.
2. How many cases match, in any queue or status. While you search, the tabs make way for the results.
3. **Back to …** returns to the tab you were on.
4. Each case shows its status, so you can tell which queue it sits in…
5. …here **Under validation** and **Documents submitted**.

If more cases match than are shown, the page says so — type more of the name or reference.

## Case statuses

| Status | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Awaiting documents** | Enrolled; the patient has not sent the documents yet. |
| **Documents submitted** | The documents arrived. The case waits in **New requests**. |
| **Under validation** | A coordinator is checking it. Opening a submitted case moves it here. |
| **Correction requested** | Sent back to the patient with a note; waiting for them. |
| **Resubmitted** | The patient sent the corrections; it waits for you in **Under validation**. |
| **Approved** | The coupon is active and the doses can be delivered. |
| **Rejected** | Final. The patient can only come back through a new enrolment. |
| **Cancelled** | The coupon was cancelled after approval. |
| **Closed — window expired** | The upload deadline passed before the case was validated. |

## Open and check a case

### 1. Read the case before you open it

::shot case-unowned | A case nobody has opened yet.
1. The patient's name, the programme and how the patient was enrolled — when, by whom and under which doctor.
2. The case status.
3. *Nobody owns this case.* You can read everything, but you cannot act yet.
4. **Open case** locks the case to you.
5. This warning appears when the patient also has a live case on another programme of your office.
6. **Abuse signals on this case** — raised by the platform; see step 7.
7. **Back to worklist** returns to the tab you opened the case from.

### 2. Open the case — it locks to you

::shot case-locked | The case is now locked to you.
1. *This case is locked to you.* The countdown shows when the release timer frees it.
2. **Release case** hands the case back to the queue straight away, if you stop working on it.
3. Opening a submitted case moves it to **Under validation**.
4. **Approve**…
5. …**Request correction**…
6. …and **Reject** appear only while the case is locked to you.

> [!warn] Nothing is saved until you submit a decision. If you leave the case, release it, or the timer runs out, anything you typed is lost. In the last two minutes the panel turns orange and asks you to *finish and submit now*. Once the lock has released, the decision buttons disappear; open the case again to continue.

Your Operator Admin sets the length of the release timer for each programme.

### 3. Check the patient and the programme rules

::shot case-records | The patient, and the programme's rules on the day of enrolment.
1. **National ID** — partly hidden. Click the eye to show the full number when you need it.
2. **ID card** — **View front** and **View back** open the photos of the patient's ID card.
3. **Patient consent** — the version the patient accepted and when; **View consent** opens it.
4. **Program rules at enrollment** — the rules this case runs under, frozen on the day of enrolment. Later changes to the programme do not change them.
5. **Strength list price at enrollment (reference only)** — for reference; *Not recorded at enrollment* when no price was recorded.
6. **Upload deadline** — the last day for the patient's documents.

You also see the mobile number, date of birth, governorate and region, and the diagnosis.

### 4. Look at the ID card

::shot id-card | The ID card photo.
1. **National ID card — front** (or back), with the patient's name.
2. Compare the photo with the patient's name and national ID.
3. Close it with **×** or the Esc key.

If the patient has not uploaded the card, the case says *No card images on file*.

### 5. Check the consent

::shot consent | The consent, exactly as the patient accepted it.
1. **Consent — version …** — the wording frozen at the moment the patient accepted it.
2. Who accepted it — with the national ID partly hidden (the eye shows it), the phone number, the date and the time.
3. The consent text in Arabic…
4. …and in English.
5. **Print or save as PDF** prints the record. The printed copy always keeps the national ID partly hidden.

### 6. Read the answers and the documents

::shot case-documents | What you validate: the answers and the documents.
1. **Enrollment answers** — the doctor's answers, on the version of the form the case was enrolled with.
2. **Documents** — everything the patient uploaded.
3. **Required test** — a test the programme asked for.
4. **Medical** (or **Financial**) — the kind of document.
5. Click a file name to preview it on this page; **Open in new tab** is inside the preview. Each file shows when it was uploaded.

### 7. Keep abuse signals in view

::shot abuse-signals | Abuse signals on the case.
1. **Abuse signals on this case**, and how many are open.
2. The platform raises them on its own. Nothing is blocked — the MedTech admin decides what to do.
3. Each signal: what was noticed, who it is about (**Patient**, **Doctor** or **Pharmacy**) and when. **Under review** means a MedTech admin is already looking at it.
4. **Show … resolved** opens the signals that are already closed, with the action taken.

You cannot change a signal. Keep it in mind when you take your decision.

### 8. Add a note to the case history

::shot case-history | The case history and the note box.
1. Filter the history: **All**, **System**, **Decisions**, **Notes** or **Pharmacy**. Each shows how many entries it holds.
2. Each entry shows the time, who did it and what happened, newest first.
3. Notes are part of the audit record: they can never be edited or deleted, and they must never contain a clinical opinion.
4. Type your note — for example, the outcome of a phone call.
5. **Save note**. Notes need the case lock, like every decision.

### A case another coordinator holds

::shot case-locked-other | A case locked to someone else.
1. You can read the whole case.
2. The panel names the coordinator who holds it and says when the release timer frees it. Until then there is no **Open case** button.

Rejected, cancelled and closed cases show no **Open case** button either: there is nothing left to decide.

## Decide on a case

Every decision needs the case lock. Each one opens a window where you confirm it; **Cancel** closes the window without deciding.

### Approve

::shot approve | Approving a case on a pharmacy programme.
1. **Approve this case?** The coupon activates with the programme's default expiry, and the first dose is issued.
2. The patient's home governorate and region — choose the closest branch.
3. **Dispensing pharmacy** — every branch of the programme, with the number of boxes it holds. For a patient a pharmacy enrolled, that branch is already chosen; you can change it.
4. Approval reserves one box at that branch, unless the programme lets the pharmacist type the box serial.
5. **Note for the case history (optional)** — written into the history under your name, for good.
6. **Approve case** confirms. You return to the worklist and the case moves to **Approved**.

If no branch is attached to the programme, or the chosen branch has no box, the window says so before you confirm. On a home-nurse programme there is no pharmacy to choose. Some programmes also let you approve and choose the pharmacy later — see [Programmes with advanced pharmacy search](#programmes-with-advanced-pharmacy-search).

### Approve with a co-payment tier

::shot approve-tier | A programme with co-payment tiers.
1. **Co-payment tier** — pick the tier you assessed. Each shows the patient's discount and what the patient pays.
2. **Note for the case history (optional)**.
3. **Approve case**.

> [!note] If the patient has not accepted the programme's consent in the app yet, the approval is refused. Approve once they have.

### Send back for a correction

::shot correction | Sending the case back to the patient.
1. The case goes to the patient and comes back to **Under validation** when they resubmit — never to **New requests**.
2. **Reason** — pick one from the programme's list.
3. **Note to the patient** — required.
4. The patient reads exactly this note. Say what has to change.
5. **Send back** confirms. The status becomes **Correction requested**.

### Reject

::shot reject | Rejecting a case.
1. **Rejection is FINAL.** The only way back for this patient is a new enrolment.
2. **Reason** — pick one from the programme's list.
3. If you pick **Other**, **Describe the reason** appears and is required.
4. **Reject case** confirms. You return to the worklist and the case moves to **Rejected**.

### A resubmitted case, and requests for more time

::shot resubmitted | A resubmitted case whose patient also asked for more time.
1. **Resubmitted** — the patient sent the corrected documents.
2. *The patient asked for more upload time* — when they asked, the current deadline and their reason.
3. One request per case — your answer is final.
4. **Grant extension** moves the upload deadline; **Refuse extension** keeps it. Both ask you to confirm.
5. Decide on the case itself as usual: **Approve**, **Request correction** or **Reject**.
6. The correction you asked for last time.

The request shows even before you open the case, so you know what you are opening it for. Patients who asked for more time also wait in the **Extension requests** tab.

## Programmes with advanced pharmacy search

On some programmes you can ask pharmacies whether they hold the medicine before you assign one. On these programmes an approved case without a pharmacy shows a **Pharmacy search** card. Everywhere else nothing changes: you choose the pharmacy when you approve.

> [!note] A request is a question. It assigns nothing and reserves nothing, and the pharmacy never sees who the patient is — only the medicine, the strength and the quantity.

### Approve now, choose the pharmacy later

::shot search-approve | Approving on a programme with advanced pharmacy search.
1. **Dispensing pharmacy** — choose a branch as usual, or **Decide the pharmacy later**.
2. *You can ask pharmacies for availability after approving.*
3. **Approve case**. The coupon becomes active, but no dose is issued until a pharmacy is assigned. You stay on the case.

Deciding later is optional: you can still choose a branch here. On a programme without advanced pharmacy search there is no such option, and a pharmacy is required to approve.

### A case waiting for a pharmacy

Approving hands the lock back: press **Open case** again to work on it.

::shot search-no-pharmacy | An approved case with no pharmacy yet.
1. **Pharmacy search** — asking assigns nothing, and the patient's details are never shared with a pharmacy.
2. *No pharmacy has been asked yet.*
3. **Ask pharmacies** sends availability requests.
4. **Offer the patient a choice** — once two or more pharmacies have confirmed.
5. **Mark as unfulfilled** — when no pharmacy can supply the medicine.
6. *Awaiting pharmacy assignment. No dose is issued until a pharmacy is assigned.* There is no delivery code yet.
7. **Assign pharmacy** assigns any pharmacy of the programme straight away, without asking first.

Asking is never required: you can assign a pharmacy at any time.

### Ask pharmacies

::shot search-ask | Asking pharmacies whether they hold the medicine.
1. Each pharmacy you tick is asked. It sees the medicine, the strength and the quantity only — never the patient.
2. A pharmacy whose request is still waiting or confirmed cannot be asked twice; its box is greyed out.
3. Tick the pharmacies to ask. A pharmacy that refused, or whose request you withdrew, can be asked again.
4. **Ask … pharmacies** sends the requests.

If a pharmacy enrolled the patient, it is ticked for you. It still has to reply like any other.

### Follow the replies

::shot search-requests | The requests on the case.
1. **Confirmed** — the pharmacy says it has the medicine.
2. **Waiting** — no reply yet. **Waiting for** shows for how long.
3. A refused request shows the pharmacy's reason, for example **Out of stock**, and its note if it wrote one.
4. **Assign** this pharmacy.
5. **Withdraw** a waiting or confirmed request.
6. **Offer the patient a choice** among the confirmed pharmacies.

| State | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Waiting** | Sent; the pharmacy has not replied yet. |
| **Confirmed** | The pharmacy holds the medicine. Nothing is reserved for this patient. |
| **Refused** | With a reason — **Out of stock**, **Does not stock this medicine** or **Other** — and sometimes a note. You can ask again. |
| **Withdrawn** | You withdrew the request. You can ask again. |
| **Closed** | A pharmacy was assigned to the case, so the request is no longer needed. |

You can send more requests at any time while the case has no pharmacy.

### Offer the patient a choice

**Offer the patient a choice** lists the pharmacies that confirmed, all ticked. Untick any you do not want to offer, keep at least two, and press **Send the offer**. To use only one pharmacy, assign it directly.

::shot search-offered | An offer waiting for the patient.
1. *Offered to the patient: …* The patient chooses one of these pharmacies in the MedTech app, and the choice assigns it.
2. **Withdraw** takes the offer back while the patient has not chosen.

A new offer replaces the one already open, and withdrawing a confirmed request removes that pharmacy from the offer. You can still assign a pharmacy yourself. The case history records every request, reply and offer; the patient's choice appears there under **The patient**.

### Assign a pharmacy

Press **Assign** on a confirmed row, or **Assign pharmacy** on the Delivery codes card to choose any pharmacy of the programme, asked or not. **Assign this pharmacy?** asks you to confirm: the dose code is issued, the patient is told, and every other request on the case closes.

::shot search-assigned | After a pharmacy is assigned.
1. *A pharmacy is assigned. The requests below are kept as history.*
2. The other requests are **Closed**.
3. The first dose is **Ready for collection** at the assigned pharmacy.
4. **Reassign pharmacy** works as on any approved case.

The same happens when the patient chooses a pharmacy from your offer.

### When no pharmacy can supply it

::shot search-unfulfilled-note | Marking the case as unfulfilled.
1. Use it when no pharmacy can supply the medicine for now. The patient and the doctor are told the medicine is delayed. Nothing is blocked.
2. **Note** — required. Say what was tried and what is expected next.
3. **Mark as unfulfilled**.

::shot search-unfulfilled | A case marked as unfulfilled.
1. **Approved, Unfulfilled**, with your note and when it was marked.
2. **Clear** removes the mark once things move again.
3. Requests carry on: you can still ask, offer and assign.

The mark also goes away by itself when a pharmacy is assigned.

::shot search-worklist | The mark in the worklist.
1. **Unfulfilled** under the patient's name, in the **Approved** tab. There is no separate queue for these cases.
2. Going back to the worklist does not release the case: it stays **Yours** until you release it or the timer frees it.

## After approval

An approved case stays in **Approved** while its doses are collected. Open it to take the lock:

::shot approved-actions | An approved case, opened.
1. **Approved**.
2. **Move collection date** gives the patient more time to collect the current dose.
3. **Cancel coupon** withdraws the patient's remaining doses.
4. **Follow-ups** — the doctor's follow-up results, when the programme asks for them.

### Review a follow-up

::shot follow-ups | Follow-ups on an approved case.
1. **Validated** — a follow-up already reviewed, with who reviewed it and when.
2. **Awaiting review** — a follow-up the doctor submitted.
3. **Proposed strength change** — the doctor proposed a new strength; it applies once you validate this follow-up. The doctor's notes and answers are shown with it.
4. **Validate** confirms the results and unlocks the next dose's delivery code.
5. **Reject** sends it back: the next dose stays locked until an acceptable follow-up is submitted.

Both buttons open a confirmation with an optional note.

### Delivery codes and the dispensing pharmacy

::shot delivery-codes | One delivery code per dose.
1. **Reassign pharmacy** — moves the case to another branch, for example when a branch runs out of stock or closes.
2. **Received** — the dose was handed over, with the branch and the time.
3. **Ready for collection** — the patient can collect this dose now.
4. **Collectable** — the dates this dose can be collected.

::shot reassign | Reassigning the dispensing pharmacy.
1. The chosen branch dispenses the next dose. The box reserved at the current branch goes back to its stock.
2. The patient's home governorate and region.
3. **Pharmacy** — pick the new branch.
4. **Reassign** confirms.

Reassigning needs the case lock. It is not offered on home-nurse programmes, or once every dose has been delivered.

| Dose status | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Not issued yet** | The code is not issued yet — for example, a follow-up must be validated first, or no pharmacy is assigned yet. |
| **Ready for collection** | The patient can collect it at the assigned branch. |
| **At the counter** | A pharmacist has the code open right now. |
| **Awaiting patient confirmation** | The pharmacy submitted the handover; the patient has not confirmed on their phone. |
| **Received** | Handed over. |
| **Awaiting visit scheduling** / **Visit scheduled** | Home-nurse doses: waiting for the patient to book, or booked. |
| **Expired** / **Cancelled** | The code can no longer be used. |

### Release a code the patient never confirmed

::shot locked-code | A dose waiting for the patient's confirmation.
1. **Awaiting patient confirmation** — the pharmacy submitted this dose, but the patient did not confirm on their phone.
2. It stays locked until someone releases it.
3. **Release code** asks for a **Reason for the release** (required, kept in the audit record). The dose goes back to **Ready for collection** so the patient can collect it again.

Releasing a code does not need the case lock.

### Move the collection date

::shot collection-date | Giving the patient more time to collect.
1. The move is recorded with its reason.
2. **New collection date** — later than today only.
3. **Reason** — required.
4. **Move the date** confirms.

Use it, for example, from the **Expiring soon** tab when a patient cannot reach the pharmacy before the coupon expires. Nothing is ever extended without you.

### Cancel a coupon

::shot cancel-coupon | Cancelling an approved coupon.
1. Undelivered doses are withdrawn; doses already delivered keep their money records. The patient is told the reason; the doctor is told his coupon moved.
2. **Reason** — required, from the programme's list.
3. **Note (optional)** — required only for the reason **Other**.
4. Whether a subsidy was already delivered, and whether the place returns to the doctor.
5. **Cancel coupon** confirms. The case becomes **Cancelled** and you return to the worklist.

## Complete the form of a pharmacy or coordinator enrolment

When a pharmacy or a coordinator enrols a patient, only the identity and the programme are entered. A coordinator then completes the clinical enrolment form on the case. Until then, no decision is possible.

::shot form-pending | A case whose form is still pending.
1. The patient's name.
2. **Form pending**.
3. When and by whom the patient was enrolled — here, a pharmacy branch.
4. **Open case** to take the lock.
5. *This patient was enrolled without the clinical form; complete it before validating.*
6. *Open the case to complete its form.*

::shot form-complete | Completing the form.
1. **Diagnosis**.
2. **Prescribed strength**.
3. Answer every question of the programme form. A red star marks a required one.
4. Tick each test the patient must upload.
5. **Save the form** works once nothing is missing; the line above it counts what is left.

After saving you see *Form completed — the case can now be validated.* Saving also hands the lock back: open the case again when it is ready for your decision. The patient still has to upload the documents in the app.

## Enrol a patient yourself

**Enrol a patient** appears in the menu only if your Operator Admin allows you to enrol. You confirm who the patient is and pick the programme; the patient finishes in the app, and the clinical form is completed from the case. One place is spent when you submit.

::shot enrol-programme | Choose the programme.
1. **Enrol a patient**.
2. **Programme** — open the list.
3. Each programme shows how many places you have left.

::shot enrol-new-patient | A patient the platform does not know yet.
1. **Patient national ID** — the 14 digits on the patient's ID card.
2. **Check** looks the patient up.
3. *No patient with this ID* — so you create a short profile.
4. **Patient full name** — as on the ID card.
5. **Patient mobile number** — the patient's own mobile.
6. **Send code** texts a confirmation code to that mobile.

::shot enrol-code | Confirm with the code from the patient's phone.
1. The code is on its way (a message shows the number it went to). If it does not arrive, **Send again** when the countdown ends.
2. **Confirmation code** — the patient reads it to you from their phone.
3. **Enrol & send onboarding SMS** enrols the patient and texts them how to continue in the app.
4. The patient finishes in the app; the clinical form is completed from the case.

::shot enrol-done | The new case.
1. *Enrolled — case …* The new case opens.
2. Its source is **Operator portal**, with the date and who enrolled the patient.
3. **Form pending**.
4. Open the case and complete its form, as described above.

If the patient is already known to MedTech:

::shot enrol-known-patient | A patient MedTech already knows.
1. **Patient found**.
2. The patient's name, Arabic name and area, and their phone number partly hidden — compare them with the ID card.
3. **Use this patient**, then **Send code**: the code goes to the mobile number on file. Continue as above.

> [!note] A patient who already has a live case on the programme cannot be enrolled on it again: the screen stops at the ID step and names that case. If you have no places left, ask your Operator Admin for more.

## Near expirations

A daily list of patients to call about boxes they received whose expiry date is near. It is not a pharmacy stock list.

::shot near-expirations | Near expirations.
1. **Program** — show one programme or all of them.
2. **Export (anonymized)** downloads the list without patient names…
3. …and **Export (identifiable)** with them.
4. The patient's name, phone and case reference. Click the name to open the case, and note what the patient told you in the case history.
5. **Expires this month**, **Expired** or **Expiry unknown**. A **Free dose** is followed up too.
6. **Mark Contacted** once you have reached the patient.
7. **Contacted** — this follow-up is done.

An unanswered call stays open: leave the row as it is. Every coordinator of your office can read and export the list; calling, notes and **Mark Contacted** also need the programme assignment and the case lock.

## Nurse visits

For home-nurse programmes, the visits of your cases.

::shot nurse-visits | Nurse visits.
1. **Status** — show the visits in one state, or **All states**.
2. Each visit's status, for example **Visit scheduled**.
3. The visit address is stored on the visit only, never copied to the patient profile.
4. **When the nurse cannot reach the patient** — the steps to follow.
5. The system never treats a missed contact as a cancellation, a failure or a delivery.

This page is for reading only: the patient confirms the visit in the app.

## Notifications

::shot notifications | Notifications.
1. **Mark all as read** clears the unread dots.
2. Show **All**, or only the **Unread** ones.
3. Each notification says what happened and when. A dot marks an unread one; click it to mark it as read.

You are told, for example, when a new case arrives for validation, when a doctor asks for more places and when a coupon expires tomorrow.

## Quick answers

**I opened a case by mistake.** Click **Release case**. It goes straight back to the queue for the next coordinator.

**My timer ran out while I was typing.** The decision can no longer be sent. Copy any text you need, open the case again and start the decision again.

**Can I undo a rejection?** No. Rejection is final; the patient needs a new enrolment.

**The patient's documents are not readable.** Use **Request correction** and say in the note exactly what to upload again.

**No pharmacy has the medicine.** On a programme with advanced pharmacy search, use **Mark as unfulfilled** with a note; the patient and the doctor are told the medicine is delayed.

**A pharmacy says the patient left without confirming.** Open the case and use **Release code** on that dose, with the reason.

**Who sees what I did?** Every decision, note and release is recorded in the case history under your name, with its time.
