## Before you start

The pharmacy portal is a web page. It works on a phone, a tablet or a computer; a phone is easiest at the counter because it can scan the patient's QR code.

- **Your sign-in.** MedTech gives each branch one sign-in: a phone number and a password. It belongs to the branch, not to one person, so everyone at the counter uses it. If the password is lost, ask MedTech to reset it.
- **Keep it on your home screen.** On a phone, open the portal in the browser and choose *Add to Home screen* from the browser menu. It then opens like an app.
- **What the patient brings.** Their phone with the MedTech patient app (it shows the delivery code and QR), their national ID card, and — from the second dose — the box of the previous dose.

> [!note] You only ever see what the handover needs: the patient's name, their ID card and national ID number, and the dose. Medical records stay with the programme team.

## Sign in

::shot sign-in | The sign-in screen.
1. **Phone number** — the branch's phone number.
2. **Password** — the branch's password.
3. Tap the eye to show the password while you type it.
4. **Sign in** opens the counter.
5. Switch the screen between English and العربية at any time.

The portal keeps you signed in on this device. Use **Sign out** (in the settings menu, below) when you leave a shared device. **How to use this portal**, under the sign-in box, opens this guide.

## The counter screen

Everything starts here. The counter screen shows the box for the patient's code and the list of doses waiting at your branch.

::shot counter-home | The counter screen after you sign in.
1. **Scan QR** opens the camera to scan the QR code on the patient's phone.
2. Or type the patient's **Delivery Code** here…
3. …and tap **Open**.
4. **Waiting at this branch** lists every dose assigned to your branch that is ready for collection.
5. Search the list by patient name or national ID.
6. Tap a patient to open their dose directly. *Ready* means it can be collected now.
7. The menu opens the other pages of the portal.

> [!tip] When you scan, hold the QR code inside the frame — it opens on its own. If the camera does not start, allow camera access in the browser or tap **Type the code instead**.

The gear at the top opens the settings menu.

::shot settings | The settings menu.
1. **Language** — English or العربية.
2. **Theme** — light or dark screen.
3. **Sign out** — always sign out on a shared device.

**User guide**, just above **Sign out**, opens this guide in a new tab.

::shot menu | The menu.
1. **Redeem coupon** — the counter screen.
2. **Enrol patient** — only shown if the programme allows your branch to enrol patients.
3. **Stock** — the box serials your branch holds and has handed over.
4. **Requests** — programme coordinators asking whether you have a medicine. The red dot counts the ones waiting for your reply.
5. **Branch account** — who this sign-in is.
6. **Notifications** — alerts about doses at your branch. A red dot counts the unread ones.

## Hand over a dose, step by step

The whole handover happens on one screen, from top to bottom. Nothing is saved until the patient confirms on their phone, so if you stop half way nothing is recorded.

### 1. Open the dose and read it

::shot dose-open | The dose, opened from the patient's code.
1. The patient's name. Check it is the person in front of you.
2. **Drug** — the medicine and strength to hand over.
3. **Dose** — which dose this is (for example *Dose 2 of 3*).
4. **Code valid until** — the last day this code can be used.
5. **Price due** — the full price of the dose. The amount the patient pays is shown further down, after any discount.
6. **Cancel** leaves without recording anything.

Below these you also see the codes, the collection **Window** (the dates this dose can be collected) and whether the visit's location was recorded.

### 2. Check the patient's identity

::shot identity | The identity check.
1. The national ID number, partly hidden. Compare it with the ID card the patient hands you.
2. Tap the eye to show the full number when you need it.
3. Tick **I checked the patient's ID against this number.** You cannot continue without it.

The ID card picture the patient uploaded and their full Arabic name are shown above, to compare with the card.

> [!warn] If the screen says the identity record is incomplete, there is no number to check against. Do not hand over — ask the programme coordinator to fix the case.

### 3. Record the previous box (from the second dose)

::shot packaging | The previous dose's packaging.
1. **Previous dose's packaging** — choose what the patient brought back: **Returned**, **Returned damaged**, or **Cannot produce it**.

The previous box's serial is shown under the box serial field, so you can check the returned blister against it.

> [!note] If the patient cannot produce the previous box, this dose is at full price — the exact amount appears after you submit. It never stops the handover, and the patient stays in the programme.

### 4. Enter the serial of the box you hand over

::shot box-serial | The box serial and its batch.
1. **Box Serial** — type the number printed on the box you are handing over. Type it; never scan it.
2. The batch appears: batch number, batch price, expiry and distributor. Check they match the box.

Some programmes keep a list of the serials your branch holds. Then you pick the box from **Box Serial (choose from this branch's list)** instead of typing; if the box is missing from the list, use **The box isn't in the list? Type its serial instead**.

Some programmes do not track box serials at all. Then there is no **Box Serial** field:

::shot untracked-dose | A dose on a programme without box serials.
1. *This programme does not track box serials.* — so you record the pack instead.
2. **Expiry printed on the box** — type the expiry date from the pack.
3. **Lot number (optional)** — the lot or batch number on the pack, if you can read it.
4. The distributor is recorded as "not sure"; the programme team can correct it later.

> [!warn] If the batch expires within 30 days, the screen warns you. You can still hand it over, but only after you tick **I checked the box and I am handing it over despite the expiry warning.** If the screen says the serial is not registered or the box was already sold, take another box.

### 5. Read the payment message, then submit

::shot payment-message | The payment message and the submit button.
1. The payment message tells you exactly what to do about money for this dose.
2. **Submit — patient confirms next** sends the handover to the patient's phone.

The message is always one of these:

| Message | What you do |
|---|---|
| **PAYMENT IS DUE FOR THIS DOSE** | Collect the amount shown (after discount) through your usual process. |
| **PAYMENT WILL BE MADE THROUGH THE APP** | Do not collect any money. The patient pays in the app. |
| **THIS IS A FREE DOSE — NO PAYMENT DUE** | Collect nothing. |

In every case, do not hand over the medicine until the patient has confirmed on their phone.

### 6. Wait for the patient to confirm

::shot waiting | Waiting for the patient.
1. **Waiting for the patient to confirm** — do not hand over the medicine yet.
2. The amount to collect, if this dose is paid at the counter.
3. **What the patient sees on their phone** — show this to the patient if they cannot find the button.

The patient presses the button in their app. For doses paid in the app, the payment completes the confirmation on its own. Tap **Check now** to refresh.

You do not have to wait on this screen: **Back to worklist** lets you serve the next patient. The code stays locked and waits in the list as *Waiting for patient* — open it again from there. If the confirmation never arrives, your Program Operator can release the code.

### 7. Damage the box and hand it over

::shot hand-over | Confirmed — the last step.
1. **Confirmed — damage the box, then hand it over.** The dose is recorded as received.
2. The record of the handover: when it was opened, submitted and confirmed.
3. In front of the patient, cut off the barcode or punch a hole through the carton, so the box cannot be sold again — then tick **I damaged the box — barcode destroyed, serial still readable.** Keep the serial readable: the patient brings this box back next time.
4. **Next patient** returns to the counter.

## When the code does not open

::shot unknown-code | A code that is not waiting at this branch.
1. The reason the code did not open.
2. Check the code you typed against the patient's phone.

| You see | What it means |
|---|---|
| *No delivery code with this number is waiting at this pharmacy.* | The code is mistyped, or the dose is not assigned to your branch. Check it with the patient. |
| *This delivery code is not collectable at this pharmacy branch.* | The dose belongs to another branch. |
| *This dose was already handed over.* | It has been collected. Nothing more to do. |
| *This delivery code has expired or was cancelled.* | The patient must contact the programme; only the coordinator can reissue it. |
| *This dose is not collectable yet* | Its collection window has not opened. Tell the patient the date shown. |
| *Submit did not go through. Try again.* | The connection dropped. Try again; nothing was recorded. |

## Stock and box serials

::shot stock | Stock & Box Serials.
1. **Stock & Box Serials** — the boxes your branch holds for programme patients, and the ones it handed over.
2. Search by box serial or programme.

This page only shows; it never changes stock. Stock moves when you hand over a dose at the counter.

## Availability requests

On some programmes, the programme coordinator asks pharmacies whether they have a medicine before choosing one for a patient. These questions arrive under **Requests**.

A request is only a question. You never see who the patient is, and confirming does not send the patient to you. If the coordinator — or the patient — then chooses your branch, the dose appears in **Waiting at this branch** as usual.

::shot requests | Requests.
1. *Confirming does not assign a patient to you.*
2. **Waiting for your reply** — each request shows the medicine, its strength, the quantity and when it was sent.
3. **We have it** — confirm that your branch can supply it.
4. **We do not have it** — refuse, with a reason.
5. **Answered** — your earlier replies, with their state.

::shot requests-refuse | Refusing a request.
1. **Reason** — **Out of stock**, **We do not stock this medicine**, or **Other**.
2. **Note (optional)** — anything the coordinator should know.
3. **Send reply**.

> [!note] A reply is final. If things change, the coordinator can ask again. A request the coordinator withdrew, or that closed because another pharmacy was chosen, shows as *Withdrawn* or *Closed*.

## Branch account

::shot account | Branch account.
1. **Branch account** — the branch, the display name and the role of this sign-in.
2. **One account per branch.** Every handover made with this sign-in is recorded against the branch, not against one pharmacist.

## Notifications

::shot notifications | Notifications.
1. **Mark all as read** clears the unread dots. Use *All* and *Unread* to filter.

You are told, for example, when a dose is waiting on its patient or when a patient presents an expired code.

## Enrol a new patient

Only if the programme lets your branch enrol patients does **Enrol patient** appear in the menu. You enter who the patient is; the patient then finishes in their app and the programme coordinator completes the enrolment form.

::shot enrol-programme | Choose the programme.
1. Pick the programme. *places left* is how many more patients your branch can enrol on it.

::shot enrol-id | Enter the national ID.
1. **National ID** — the 14 digits on the patient's card.
2. **Check** looks the patient up.

If the patient is new to MedTech:

::shot enrol-new-patient | A patient the platform does not know yet.
1. *No patient with this ID* — so you enter their details.
2. **Patient name** — as on the ID card.
3. **Mobile number** — the patient's own mobile; the code goes to it.
4. **Send code** texts a confirmation code to that mobile.

::shot enrol-code | Confirm with the code from the patient's phone.
1. **Confirm and enrol** — check the programme, name and ID once more.
2. Shows which number the code went to. If it does not arrive, send it again after the countdown.
3. **Confirmation code** — ask the patient to read you the 6-digit code.
4. **Enrol & send onboarding SMS** enrols the patient and texts them how to continue in the app.

::shot enrol-done | Enrolled.
1. **Enrolled** — the case reference is shown under it.
2. The patient finishes in the app; the coordinator completes the form. Use **Enrol another patient** for the next one.

If the patient is already known to MedTech, you only confirm it is them:

::shot enrol-known-patient | A patient MedTech already knows.
1. **Patient found.**
2. **Is this the patient?** The name is shortened on purpose — compare it with the card.
3. **Yes — send the code** sends the confirmation code to the patient's registered mobile. Then continue as above.

> [!note] A patient who already has a live case on the programme cannot be enrolled on it again; the screen stops at the ID step and shows the case reference. If no places are left, ask the programme to give your branch more.

## Quick answers

**Can I hand over before the patient confirms?** No. Always wait for *Confirmed — damage the box, then hand it over*.

**The patient's phone is dead.** The patient must confirm on their own phone. Ask them to come back once it is charged; the dose stays waiting.

**I opened the wrong patient.** Tap **Cancel**. Nothing is recorded until you submit.

**I submitted but the patient left.** The code stays locked as *Waiting for patient*. Your Program Operator can release it.

**Who sees what I did?** Every handover is recorded against your branch with the time it was opened, submitted and confirmed.
